Five warning signs of heart disease

Medically reviewed by Dr. Nguyen Van An

Chest pain or pressure, shortness of breath, unusual fatigue, dizziness and swollen ankles can all signal heart problems. Do not wait for symptoms to disappear: if pain spreads to the arm, jaw or back, or comes with sweating, call 115 immediately. Risk grows with high blood pressure, diabetes, smoking and family history. A yearly check-up with blood pressure, cholesterol and ECG tests allows doctors to find problems early. Regular exercise, less salt and no tobacco remain the most effective prevention.

Preparing your child for vaccination

Medically reviewed by Dr. Tran Thi Binh

Bring the vaccination record book and tell the nurse about allergies, fever or recent illness. Keep the child hydrated and dress them in loose clothing. Mild fever and a sore arm for one or two days are common. Stay in the waiting area for 30 minutes after the injection so staff can respond to rare reactions. Call the clinic if fever lasts longer than two days or the child is unusually drowsy.

What to expect during a pregnancy check-up

Medically reviewed by Dr. Le Minh Chau

Antenatal visits track the mother's blood pressure, weight and the baby's growth. The first trimester includes blood tests and an early ultrasound; the second includes a detailed anatomy scan; the third focuses on birth planning. Report bleeding, severe headache, reduced fetal movement or leaking fluid at once. Take folic acid and iron as advised and avoid alcohol and unprescribed medicine.

How to prepare for an endoscopy

Medically reviewed by Dr. Do Anh Khoa

Follow the fasting instructions given at booking, usually no food for six hours. Tell the doctor about medicines, especially blood thinners, and any allergies. Bring a companion if sedation is planned and do not drive for the rest of the day. The procedure normally takes 15 to 30 minutes, and results are explained after you recover.
